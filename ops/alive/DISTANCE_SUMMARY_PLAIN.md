# How far is Earth-1 from real people? (plain language, 2026-09-02)

**When you ask Earth-1 what a country thinks, it lands on the same side as the real
human majority 83.5% of the time** — across 21,776 question-country pairs it has
never been trained on, with the abstention rule on (it declines questions about
named people and institutions its world has never heard of, ~500 cells).

**But "right side of the majority" is a coarse bar.** Measured against the survey's
own precision — could a pollster tell Earth-1's answer from the real one? — only
14.2% of answers are indistinguishable from the human sample. The typical answer
sits 3.8 sampling-errors from the human number (about 10.7 percentage points).

**Where it's closest:** technology and climate attitudes (3.0σ), Oceania (2.3σ).
**Where it's farthest:** religious belief (3.9σ) and East Asia (4.7σ) — the same
two gaps the input campaign already targets (religiosity and regional value inputs).
The signed errors say the model is not systematically biased one direction overall;
each family has its own tilt (in the family table).

**On real events:** of ten direction calls on COVID, the financial crisis and the
Arab Spring, four match what happened; most of the rest are statistically
indistinguishable from no-response at current replication — the engine's event
responses only became measurable at all this week. Its one strong external result
stands: it ranks WHERE protests happen with ρ=0.55 (p=0.005) against real recorded
onsets.

**One sentence:** Earth-1 usually agrees with humanity's majority, is rarely yet as
precise as a real poll, knows geography better than magnitude — and every number
above comes with the baseline columns and raw cells committed beside it.
